Re-learning biology · Part 2

The tags on the tails. What are histone marks, and how do we read them?

H3K4me3, H3K27me3, ChIP-seq peaks: what the names mean, where the marks sit, how we measure them, and what a 2023 paper showed about whether they actually do anything.

By Aathma Merin Bejoy Bioinformatician, Berlin 12 Nov 2026 ~5 min read

In Part 1 I looked at how DNA wraps around histones, and found that the textbook answer, "positive histones stick to negative DNA", is only half the story. The core is held together mostly by many hydrogen bonds and nonpolar contacts. The charged histone tails stick out and act more like adjustable hands.

Those tails are also where the cell puts most of its chemical tags. These are the marks I see in ChIP-seq data every week, so this part is about what they are, how we measure them, and how they connect to a gene being on or off.

01 · HISTONE MARKSChemical tags on the tails

Names like H3K9me3 or H3K27ac are everywhere in my work. They are chemical groups that the cell's own enzymes attach to histones after the protein is made, which is why they are called post-translational modifications (PTMs). The famous ones are acetylation, methylation, phosphorylation and ubiquitination. Since around 2011, mass spectrometry has added many more: lactylation, crotonylation, succinylation, citrullination, propionylation, butyrylation and others. A 2024 review counts nine of these "novel" types alone.1 Lactylation was found on histones in 2019, with 28 sites on the core histones in the first study.2

Five marks worth knowing first

The NIH Roadmap Epigenomics project used five core marks to describe every one of its epigenomes:3,4

Roadmap Epigenomics did for the epigenome something like what the Human Genome Project did for the sequence. It published 111 reference human epigenomes in 2015, profiled for histone modifications, DNA accessibility, DNA methylation and RNA expression.4 One thing that took me a moment: there is one human reference genome, but there is no single reference epigenome. The epigenome is the full collection of chemical tags and proteins on the DNA and its histones, and it is different in every cell and tissue type. That is why they needed 111.

▸ LATEST FINDINGS · HISTONE MARKS2023–2026
  • Nature · 2023Wang, Fan … HelinDoes H3K4me3 actually do anything? For years people argued whether H3K4me3 just marks active promoters or helps make them active. By removing it acutely in mouse embryonic stem cells, this study showed it is not needed for transcription to start, but without it RNA polymerase II pauses more and elongates more slowly. H3K4me3 recruits a protein (INTS11) that helps release the paused polymerase.5
  • eLife · 2026H3K115acMarks on the core, not only the tails. Acetylation at H3 lysine 115, which sits on the side of the nucleosome near the dyad, is enriched on "fragile" nucleosomes in nucleosome-depleted regions at promoters, active enhancers and CTCF sites, where it coincides with transcription factor binding.6
  • Signal Transduct. Target. Ther. · 2024Yao, Hu & Wang (review)The new acylations. A good overview of nine newer histone modifications (lactylation, citrullination, crotonylation, succinylation and others) and what is known about their links to disease.1

02 · METHODSHow we read the tags, and the open DNA

This is the part where the biology turns into the files I work with. There are two different questions. Where is a particular mark? That is ChIP-seq, and newer methods like CUT&Tag. Where is the DNA open? That is ATAC-seq.

ChIP-seq (chromatin immunoprecipitation followed by sequencing) uses an antibody against, for example, H3K4me3, pulls down the DNA fragments carrying it, and sequences them. It works, but it suffers from low signal, high background, epitope masking from cross-linking, and it needs a lot of cells.7

CUT&Tag (2019, Henikoff lab) keeps the antibody but turns the process inside out: the antibody binds in the cell and brings along a protein A–Tn5 transposase fusion, which cuts the DNA right there and inserts sequencing adapters. The result is high resolution and very low background, from small numbers of cells and even single cells.7

ATAC-seq (2013, Greenleaf and Chang labs) uses no antibody at all. Hyperactive Tn5 transposase inserts sequencing adapters into native chromatin wherever it can reach, which means wherever the DNA is open. It needs only 500 to 50,000 cells and also tells you about nucleosome positions and transcription factor binding.8 As someone who works in a transposon lab, I love that the key enzyme in ATAC-seq is a bacterial transposase.

▸ LATEST FINDINGS · METHODS2025
  • Nature · 2025TACIT / CoTACITSingle-cell histone marks in early embryos. TACIT gives genome-coverage single-cell profiles of seven histone modifications across mouse early embryos, from zygote to blastocyst.9 Its extension CoTACIT maps up to six marks in the same cell.10
  • Nature Methods · Sep 2025Geisenberger et al., scEpi²-seqHistone marks and DNA methylation on the same molecule. Combines sortChIC with TAPS conversion to measure histone modifications and DNA methylation at the same time in single cells.11
  • Science Advances · 2025scMTR-seqSix marks plus RNA. Profiles six histone modifications and the transcriptome in the same single cells.12
  • Nature Communications · Dec 2025sc-hPTMNo antibodies at all. A mass-spectrometry method that identifies and quantifies histone modifications in single cells across H1, H2A, H2B, H3 and H4, without choosing the marks in advance.13

03 · PUTTING IT TOGETHEROpen and closed chromatin

Here is how the nucleosomes and their tags connect to genes being on or off. When nucleosomes are packed tightly over a gene's promoter, the machinery that reads the gene cannot get in. Kornberg and Lorch showed in 1987 that a nucleosome on a promoter prevents transcription from starting, which made the nucleosome a general gene repressor.14 When the nucleosomes move or loosen, transcription factors can bind, and the gene can be read.
