    04_bench_to_datahow the data is made

    I know how the data was made, so I know where it can mislead.

    I studied bioinformatics first and then went back to the bench: PCR, qPCR, cell culture, CRISPR, knockdowns. That is why I read an experiment's design before I read its output.

    • sites recovered by PCRPCR favours some regions of the genome. So I ask whether a site is on or off given where it landed, not where the transposon prefers to land.
    • samples sequenced togetherIndex hopping moves reads between samples. I remove it before I call anything.
    • a ChIP without an IgG controlThe conclusions have to rest on replicate agreement and motif enrichment, and I say so.
    • bulk tissue from a tumourIt is a mix of tumour, stroma and immune cells. With no purity estimate to correct for that, I report it as a limit of the analysis.
    • a DNA library from leavesIt cannot detect RNA viruses, and those are most plant viruses.

    Do BulkRNABert embeddings know which tissue a cell line comes from?

    Machine learning side project at the MDC, 2026

    What I did

    BulkRNABert is a transformer model pretrained on tumour RNA-seq data. I ran it on 1,657 cancer cell lines from DepMap and asked whether its embeddings separate the 26 tissue lineages better than plain PCA on gene expression. A colleague set me this task to test my skills, and I did it alongside my lab work.

    Skills I used and learned

    Python (NumPy, pandas, scikit-learn), model inference in JAX/Haiku, Apptainer containers, SLURM. New for me: running a pretrained transformer, matching my data to a model's fixed gene vocabulary, and evaluating embeddings with cross-validation against a simple baseline.

    Outcome

    The embeddings are worse than the baseline. With a kNN classifier they reach a balanced accuracy of 0.40, and PCA on the 2,000 most variable genes reaches 0.60. The gap depends strongly on the lineage: lymphoid and lung cell lines are classified almost equally well by both, whereas pancreas, kidney and pleura are lost in the embedding.

    Limitations

    I only ran the model; I did not train or fine-tune it. I tested one way of pooling the embeddings and one layer, and one task. The model was trained on tumours and I applied it to cell lines, which may explain part of the gap.

    Does it matter where a transposon lands? Linking integration sites to reporter expression

    Mobile DNA lab, MDC

    What I did

    In a barcoded Sleeping Beauty reporter assay, every integration carries a reporter gene and a unique barcode. The barcode is read in DNA to find where the transposon landed, and in RNA to see whether the reporter is switched on. I built the pipeline from raw reads to the final call set: require the exact transposon end, align the genomic flank to the human genome, read the barcode, and count it in RNA. I then annotated each site with its gene context and tested enrichment against randomly shuffled sites.

    Skills I used and learned

    Python, R, SLURM, bedtools, GENCODE annotation, permutation tests and chi-square tests. I removed index hopping between samples, and I accepted weak calls only when independent evidence supported them, not by lowering a threshold. I also used the transposon's own biology as a quality check: Sleeping Beauty integrates at TA sites, so genuine calls should sit on one.

    Outcome

    A validated set of integration sites, each with a location and an on/off call, for the lab's further analysis. The results are unpublished and belong to the lab, so I do not describe them here.

    Limitations

    The sites are recovered by PCR, which favours some regions of the genome over others. The analysis asks whether a site is on or off given where it landed, not where the transposon prefers to land. Expression is a yes/no call that depends on a threshold.

    ChIP-seq of a transcription factor in human embryonic stem cells

    Mobile DNA lab, MDC, 2026

    What I did

    I built the analysis for a ChIP-seq experiment from raw reads to annotated binding sites: alignment, quality control, peak calling on replicates, consensus peaks, annotation against genes and repeat families, and motif analysis. Because the lab studies repeats, I kept reads that map to several places in a separate track instead of discarding them.

    Skills I used and learned

    BWA, samtools, MACS3, deepTools, HOMER, bedtools, SLURM, micromamba environments. New for me: judging whether a weak ChIP is real. I learned to use a failed replicate as a control for library bias, to compare against shuffled regions, and to let the motif result decide when promoter peaks could be artefacts.

    Outcome

    A documented pipeline with one script per step and a decision log, which the lab can rerun on the next round of samples. The biological results are unpublished and belong to the lab, so I do not describe them here.

    Limitations

    The experiment had no IgG control and the enrichment was low, so the conclusions rest on replicate agreement and motif enrichment. Reproducibility between replicates was judged by overlap, without a formal IDR analysis.

    Differential gene expression in a rat tumour model

    Bulk RNA-seq, MDC

    What I did

    I compared bulk RNA-seq libraries from tumour and normal tissue: read quality control, alignment with STAR as a cross-check, quantification with Salmon using a decoy-aware index and bias correction, and differential expression with DESeq2. I exported a ranked gene list for gene set enrichment analysis.

    Skills I used and learned

    FastQC, MultiQC, STAR, Salmon, tximport, DESeq2 with fold-change shrinkage, R. Before trusting the comparison I checked by sample correlation that the tumour libraries were independent replicates and not repeated samples of one tumour.

    Outcome

    A differential expression table and a ranked gene list ready for enrichment analysis. The results are unpublished and belong to the lab, so I do not describe them here.

    Limitations

    There were few samples per group, so only large differences can be detected. Bulk tissue mixes tumour, stroma and immune cells, and there was no purity estimate to correct for that. Non-coding RNAs were not quantified.

    GAPDH as an RNA-binding protein in acute myeloid leukemia

    Master's thesis, Max Planck Institute of Molecular Physiology, 2024 to 2025

    What I did

    GAPDH is known as a metabolic enzyme, but it also binds RNA. In my thesis I combined CLIP-seq data, which shows which RNAs GAPDH binds, with RNA-seq data after GAPDH knockdown, which shows which RNAs change, and followed up selected targets by qPCR.

    Skills I used and learned

    R workflows for RNA-seq and CLIP-seq, differential expression, qPCR, cell culture. New for me: working on one question with both bench and computer, and taking an analysis through to a published figure.

    Outcome

    The work is part of a paper in RNA Biology (2025). The study found that GAPDH binds its targets mainly in the 5' untranslated region, including its own mRNA, and stabilises them.

    Limitations

    The experiments were done in leukemia cell lines, not in patient samples.

    Looking for viruses in a plant metagenome

    Master's dissertation, Rajiv Gandhi Centre for Biotechnology and Manipal, 2021

    What I did

    I took a public shotgun sequencing dataset of wild Arabidopsis thaliana leaves and searched it for viral sequences: quality control and trimming, removal of plant reads, de novo assembly, BLASTx against a viral protein database, and taxonomic classification. I also compiled a list of new plant viruses reported in metagenomic studies from 2015 to 2021.

    Skills I used and learned

    FastQC, Trimmomatic, Bowtie2, metaSPAdes, BLAST+, MEGAN, Galaxy. This was my first complete analysis from raw reads to a result. When the search was too large for Galaxy, I learned to set up a cloud server and run BLAST from the command line.

    Outcome

    Of 55,364 assembled contigs, 4,132 were assigned to viruses, covering 13 families. With a strict score threshold three families remained, two of them bacteriophages. I found no new virus.

    Limitations

    I analysed one of the samples in the study, mostly with default settings, and nothing was validated in the lab. The data was DNA sequencing, which cannot detect the RNA viruses that make up most plant viruses.

    next.txtwhat I want to be

    Next: building the methods myself

    I have spent the past years learning to analyse data well. The next step is to build the methods themselves. I am looking for a PhD in machine learning for genomics, where I can work on one question in depth alongside people who develop such methods.

    In the long run I want to build tools that are useful in healthcare, where a patient's molecular data helps to understand their disease or choose their treatment.

    Right now I am learning to train my own deep learning models in PyTorch, and going back over the foundations. I keep a learning page for that.

    How I work

    I care about results that can be trusted. I would rather report that a method did not work than present a result I cannot defend, and I write my analyses so that someone else can run them again.

    I learn a lot from people who teach in public, such as Ming "Tommy" Tang and Manolis Kellis. Tommy Tang's writing convinced me that a bioinformatician has to understand the biology behind the data.
